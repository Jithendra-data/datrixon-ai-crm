# Business problem

B2B teams face fragmented customer history, inconsistent notes, missed follow-ups, duplicate records and optimistic forecasts. A generic CRM's price does not guarantee adoption or workflow fit. Data capture alone does not tell an employee what deserves attention.

This reference assumes multi-stakeholder enterprise selling: discovery, qualification, proposal, negotiation and a won/lost decision. Paid orders represent commercial history; open opportunities are potential business and must not be counted as revenue.

The synthetic scenario contains late close dates, low stakeholder coverage, duplicate contacts, missing ownership, critical support and inactive enterprise deals. Healthy accounts provide comparison. These are designed examples, not observed real-customer outcomes.

The response is a focused model, explainable attention scores, a decision queue and durable audit. Build-versus-buy remains contextual: integrations, security, support and engineering capacity can outweigh license savings.
